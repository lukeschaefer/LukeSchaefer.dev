The original meme riffs on a relatable idea - that starting a
project is easy, but finishing an ambitious project is very hard. Especially when there's no one to hold
your hand through the process. But now we live in an amazing future, where there hard part is made easy, and owls are plentiful! 
Depending on who you ask, this will result in either:

- The democratization of owl-related drawings. A renaissance where quality and
  quantity are improved. A rising tide lifts all boats. Everyone is an artist,

- The destruction of owl-based artists, who are now entirely redundant and will
  have to work in coal mines to make ends meet.

## Art, Expression & Compression

Let’s move away from owl sketches for a minute and look at some higher-brow
art. Take [Svetlana Zakharova in The Dying Swan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKwsG7gS2r0). Every single
aspect of the performance - the way she holds her hands, her face, where the
movements seem stiff or flowing, bouncy or strained - is a unique expression
that adds to the ultimate performance. Though there is choreography to follow,
a different dancer would give an entirely different artistic result.

This is true of lots of art - dancing, singing, acting, painting, writing. In
all of these, a single piece is built from an uncountable pile of unique and
personal choices. Some of these choices are intentional, some unintentional,
and a lot fall into the grey area between - but all of these decisions combine
to create a work that reflects the artist as an individual. Great artists
especially know how to imbue every brushstroke, gesture, pause, and vocal
inflection with intention.

In order to give the illusion of credibility, I'll sloppily borrow jargon from Information Theory. I would
suggest that human communication, whether high art, or daily standups - whatever the medium, has a surprisingly **high signal-to-noise ratio**.
It is *thick* with information that we are interested in. Not just about the message, but about the **messenger** as well. 
When there is no longer a **messenger** to relate the message to, the work immediately less interesting. 

This is why it can be exhausting to read LLM prose. It's not because the writing is technically worse
in any particular way. It's exhausting because once you realize it's not produced by 
a human, it **loses an immense amount of information**. Once it's determined to be machine-generated, all the 
superflous word choices, all the style and tone, ceases to be interesting, since it doesn't reflect a real person.
The signal-to-noise ratio drops incredibly, 
and you'd [rather just read the prompt](https://claytonwramsey.com/blog/prompt/) to gain back some information density. The signal here isn't just the raw data and the information contained - but what the authors tone, word choice, pacing, 
reveals about a real, living human - which is something (I think) we all find inherently valuable.


## Back to the owls

The circles you added in the tool above? There are only a few million combinations of those two
circles. If everyone on Earth used this toy, we’d have millions of repeated
owls (and I would have a very large OpenRouter bill). If, however, I turn the tables, and prompt **you** with
some circles, and a drawing surface - the variety of owls will be uncountable, AND each owl will be 
a little more valuable, since we know you took the time to draw it (even if it's not pretty)

I guess what I’m saying is: **go ahead, *make my day* - draw the rest of the owl:**
