The joke of the original meme is, of course, that the given instructions are *clearly* not sufficient to instruct on the difficult task
of drawing an owl.
However, in this age of prompting, it seems it doesn't quite apply anymore. Draw the circles, give some instruction - 
and AI will happily draw the rest of the owl. As long as you can draw some circles, you too can now participate in the owl scene.
Depending on who you ask, there's at least two ways this new era could shake out:

* A renaissance in owl-based art, due to the lower barrier of entry. New contributions pour in at a rate never before seen,
  from entirely new artists - and art abounds. Skill and effort are no longer gatekeepers of anything - but ***taste*** is king.
* The disenfranchisement of traditional owl-based artists, who are now useless and purposeless and ashamed and will
  have to work in coal mines and cruises to make ends meet. Owl art homogenizes into a depressing monoculture until no one cares.

Those are the primary two outcomes as far as I can see. Here's an ad I spotted on the subway which alludes to these
two paths in a single well-crafted slogan:

![NYC subway AI ad](/job-done.png)

Does the term “job” refer to some small task you're working on... *or* does it refer to your employment itself?
Is it that *THE JOB* is done... or **YOUR JOB** is done?

Now - marketers are a bunch of sneaks, and don’t mind ruffling feathers
if it gets people talking. I’m sure they carefully crafted this ambiguity just to get you riled up,
so let's not dwell on it too long.

Still, it begs the question: could your job truly be completed by one prompt? Then the only job that remains for you,
or for any of us, is *prompter*? That doesn't make much sense - since we've already established from the owl meme that
the prompt is the easy part that anyone can do!

## Art, Expression & Compression

Let’s move away from owls and AI for a minute and look at some real-deal
art. Take a gander at [Svetlana Zakharova's performance of The Dying Swan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKwsG7gS2r0).
At a high level, you can admire the choreography and the athleticism required to execute it.
But look closer - the specific way she holds her fingers during a turn, how some movements look
weightless, and some look strained. The emotional depth of the performance is revealed
through some of the smallest choices in her movements - ones that are hardly even perceptible.
Like pixels on a screen, each detail is minuscule, but combines to create a high-definition
portrait that is rich and engaging.

This is true of lots of art - dancing, singing, acting, painting, writing. In
all of these, a single work contains a hidden temple filled with the artist's unique and
personal choices. Many of these details are unintentional; a watermark of
your style and substance is embedded when you belt a note, hold a brush, or write a
contrived metaphor. Great artists will imbue each of these details with intentionality - 
but either way, the resulting work is a part of the artist, concealed.

In order to give the illusion of credibility, I'll sloppily borrow concepts from Information Theory. I would
argue that human communication has a substantially underrated **signal-to-noise ratio**. The signal isn't
just the contents of the message ("A sad dance about a dying bird") - but what the author's tone, style,
word choice, and pacing reveal about themselves, who they are, how they think - often in ways that words are
not equipped to describe. There is an intimacy associated with seeing someone's special details
that they couldn't help but reveal in their expression. In this day and age, intimacy can be hard to come by.

This is why it can be **exhausting to read LLM prose**. Not because the writing is technically worse
in any measurable way. It's simply tedious because there is no person on whom these subtle details
shed light. Once it's determined to be machine-generated, all the style and tone cease to carry any
information we could care about. The signal-to-noise ratio drops dramatically, and you'd
[rather just read the prompt](https://claytonwramsey.com/blog/prompt/) to gain back some information density
and move on with your day.

## Back to the owls

The circles you added in the tool above? There are only a few million combinations of those two
circles. That might sound like a fair bit, but if everyone on Earth used this toy, 
we’d have millions of repeated owls (and I would have a **very** large OpenRouter bill). However,
putting pen to paper (or in this case, finger to phone) results in a mathematical explosion of possibilities.
There are unfathomably more owls able to be drawn than there are atoms in the known universe. 

I guess what I'm saying is - go ahead try to draw the rest of the owl - why not? Consider yourself **prompted**. Your owl drawing
will be worth far more to me than the perfect one above - because it reveals something about you as a person... 
even if it just reveals "this guy can NOT draw an owl to save his life".
