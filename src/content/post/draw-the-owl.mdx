This meme was once a very relatable idea - often used to show that starting a
project is easy, but finishing an ambitious project is very hard. But now we
live in an amazing future, where anyone can draw the rest of the owl. Depending
on who you ask, this will result in either:

- The democratization of owl-related drawings. A renaissance where quality and
  quantity are improved. A rising tide lifts all boats. Everyone is an artist,
  and art abounds.
- The destruction of owl-based artists, who are now entirely redundant and will
  have to work in coal mines to make ends meet.

## Art, Expression & Compression

Let’s move away from owl sketches for a minute and look at some higher-brow
art. Take [Svetlana Zakharova in The Dying Swan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKwsG7gS2r0). Every single
aspect of the performance - the way she holds her hands, her face, where the
movements seem stiff or flowing, bouncy or strained - is a unique expression
that adds to the ultimate performance. Though there is choreography to follow,
a different dancer will portray it entirely differently.

This is true of lots of art - dancing, singing, acting, painting, writing. In
all of these, a single piece is built from an uncountable pile of unique and
personal choices. Some of these choices are intentional, some unintentional,
and a lot fall into the grey area between - but all of these decisions combine
to create a work that reflects the artist as an individual.

In order to give the illusion of credibility, we can borrow some language from
information theory. Human performance is extraordinarily high-entropy. It is
dense with information we care about; every signal transmitted from artist to
viewer contains choices that make the performance unique. Great artists
especially know how to imbue every brushstroke, gesture, pause, and vocal
inflection with intention.

One interesting property of information is that it can only be compressed so
far before something is lost. When it can’t be compressed much further without
sounding or looking strange, you start to expose how much information was
actually present in the original signal.

Let’s take five seconds of medium-quality speech - how many ways are there to
fill five seconds with human speech? As raw audio, that file will sit around
441 kB. As an MP3, which adds some compression, we can get that down to 80 kB
while still sounding alright. Unfortunately, that file could still contain
white noise, bird calls, or monkey screeches.

Cutting-edge AI compression designed specifically to ONLY work for speech can
compress something recognizable into under 1 kB. This will be highly
compressed, with noticeable artifacts, but from here we can reverse the
process. How many possible 1 kB files like this could exist? Roughly 10^2400.
For context, the universe contains about 10^70 atoms. That’s still not quite
literally the number of possible human utterances, but it gives a sense of the
size of the space: more than enough to fill multiple universes with humans
speaking nonstop and never have the same phrase repeated in exactly the same
way.

## Back to the owls

But your circles above? There are only a few million combinations of those two
circles. If everyone on Earth used this toy, we’d have billions of repeated
owls (and I would have a very large OpenRouter bill). Meanwhile, if I gave you
a pencil and paper, I bet you could draw an owl that you could say with
certainty is uniquely yours.

I guess what I’m saying is: go ahead, draw the rest of the owl. It might not
look as good, but it will contain vast quantities of what makes you, you -
which I think makes it worth a lot more.
